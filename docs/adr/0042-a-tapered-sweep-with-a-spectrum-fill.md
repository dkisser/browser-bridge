# The logo is a tapered sweep with a spectrum fill

The old mark was a line drawing of the Brooklyn Bridge: literal, and legible as
"a bridge" to nobody who has not been told. It carried no gesture of its own, so
there was nothing to remember. The brief was a mark with expressive shape,
restrained colour, and the same Liquid Glass material the extension UI is built
on — with purple ruled out.

**Decision:** the mark is one stroke — a single sweep that enters thin at the
lower left, swells through the apex, and tapers off to the right — filled with a
spectrum that runs `#007aff` → `#22d3ee` → `#fbbf24` → `#ff9500`. That is three
things at once and they are not separable:

- *A tapered stroke, not a uniform one.* The stroke outline is generated by
  offsetting the centreline along its own normal with a varying half-width, so
  the width swells and thins like a brush. This is the whole expressiveness, and
  it is also why the mark survives 16px: a uniform-width stroke of the same
  visual weight blurs into a smear at toolbar size, a tapered one keeps a solid
  head.
- *A spectrum that is the shape, not a fill on a shape.* The gradient is
  interpolated along the arc's own arc length (segmented quads), not across the
  canvas. A single `linearGradient` in user space leaves the whole apex one flat
  colour, which throws away the only reason to have a gradient.
- *The two brand tokens at the ends.* `#007aff` is `--primary`, `#ff9500` is
  `--amber`, so the mark is the palette rather than a picture that happens to sit
  near it.

**Two assets, not one.** `logo.svg` / `logo.png` is flat; `logo-glass.svg` /
`logo-glass.png` adds the documented Liquid Glass stack (frost, specular rim
computed as a real ring between the outline and an offset copy of it, ambient
shadow). The flat one is what ships at icon sizes. This is measured, not taste:
below about 64px the glass stack is gone, and a toolbar slot is 16px, so asking
the glass version to work there only makes it worse.

**One PNG per declared icon size**, rendered from the vector at its own pixel
size rather than downscaled from the 1024 master. A downscaled icon is a
reduction of a reduction, and the mark pays for it: the tapered tail is ~6
units wide, which at 16px is about a tenth of a pixel, so antialiasing spread
it across one pixel at roughly 12% coverage and `#007aff` stopped reading as blue
— the shipped 16px icon was a pale grey-blue smear and the mark read as yellow
only. Each small size therefore gets a **minimum stroke width of ~1.05px in its
own units**, applied as a smooth maximum rather than a hard clamp so there is no
crease in the taper. At 128px the floor lands below the natural minimum and
nothing changes, which is the check that this is an optical correction and not a
reshape. Measured after the fix, the head of `icon-16.png` is RGB (0,161,251)
against a blue-minus-red difference of +251.

**Colour is interpolated in OKLab.** RGB space is not perceptually uniform, so a
straight line between two endpoints does not stay on the hue circle: blue → teal
→ amber detours through a dull olive, and green → yellow washes out. The first
draft of the spectrum had exactly that defect and it looked like bad colour
choice. It was bad *interpolation*; OKLab keeps chroma up for the whole ramp.

**Considered options:** a container with a knocked-out symbol (rejected — every
version of it read as a generic software icon: upload arrow, padlock, folder,
shopping bag, file tray, refresh, envelope); a second element arranged around a
void (rejected — the two shapes read as clutter, and the void had to be cut from
a single solid mass to work at all); a spiral or crossing stroke (rejected —
converged on the `@` glyph and on the enso/zen motif, both of which are already
owned by other marks); AI-generated concepts (rejected — image models return
soft blobs under an abstract brief, and over-constraining them with "no
gradients, no shading" pushes the result straight back to regular geometry).
A hand-authored SVG is simply the stronger tool for a mark that has to be
legible at 16px.

**A literal rainbow, built and measured, and still declined.** The shape question
was not left implicit: a circular arch of constant width, a concentric
three-band rainbow, and an elliptical arch were all built in this same spectrum
and rendered at each declared size beside the sweep. The result is worth
recording because it cuts *against* this decision. The constant-width arch
measurably beats the sweep at 16px — it carries the full blue→amber with no
optical compensation at all, because it has no sub-pixel part to compensate,
which is exactly the weakness the sweep had to buy its way out of. On the
measurement alone the arch should have won.

It did not, for a reason the measurement cannot see. The two shapes place the
taper differently. On the sweep the taper is load-bearing: the thin end *is* the
start of the gesture, and losing it costs the brand blue, which is the whole
reason for the optical floor. On an arch the taper is decorative — the thin parts
are the two ends of a curve, and letting them fade costs nothing. So the sweep
pays a permanent cost for its expressiveness and the arch gets expressiveness
nearly free, which is not the trade the sweep was chosen for. The arch has no
direction: it is a static symmetric curve, and it arrives carrying the entire
🌈 semantic load — weather, children's app, optimism-as-cliché. The sweep has a
beginning and an end.

The honest middle was an arch with a decorative taper, keeping both. It was
built and it works. It was declined anyway, on the same reasoning: the arch is
the part the user was drawn to, and once the arch is there the taper stops being
the mark's idea and becomes a flourish on a shape someone else already owns.

The concentric three-band rainbow is worth one more line because it fails for a
reason that generalises: its 30-unit gaps are 4px at 128 and **0.6px at 16**, so
below about 32px the three bands fill in and the mark turns to mush. Any
multi-band construction has this floor, and it is the same lesson as the
sub-pixel tail, one level up.

**Consequences:** the extension build copies four icons instead of one, so
`docs/assets/` gains `icon-16/32/48/128.png` and `manifest.json` points each
declared size at its own file. This amends the single-file convention recorded
in CHANGELOG 0.1.0 ("logo centralized in `docs/assets/logo.png`; extension build
copies it automatically to `dist/icon.png`"). The SVGs are the source of truth;
the PNGs are rendered from them and should not be hand-edited. Anyone changing
the mark should re-run the size ladder rather than re-export one master, or the
optical floor is silently lost.
